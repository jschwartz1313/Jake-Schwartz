How I Got Here

I started in environmental science because I wanted to understand how energy and infrastructure decisions affect real places. Focusing on quantitative energy systems showed me that the hardest questions rarely belong to one discipline: they sit between technology, economics, regulation, geography, and policy.

I added a master's degree in information science to get better at organizing data, analyzing systems, and building tools people can actually use. Since then, my work has moved between renewable-energy siting, regulatory and geospatial screening, data operations, commercialization research, and application development.

The common thread is translation: turning messy technical information into a clearer dataset, interface, workflow, or recommendation. I am especially drawn to energy and infrastructure problems where better analysis can improve how projects are evaluated and decisions are made.
